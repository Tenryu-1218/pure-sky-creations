Welcome to Pure Sky Creations. My work spans jewelry and metalsmithing, ink painting, calligraphy, seal carving, and origami, and is shaped by both Eastern aesthetic tradition and Western fabrication craft. Each piece is made to be precise in execution and considered in form.

01 · Pieces

Four practices, one hand.

Each piece is one of a kind and is offered as shown.

Payment may be made by secure card checkout through Stripe or by Zelle. Both options are listed beneath each piece.

i. Jewelry & Metalsmithing

Sterling silver, designed and fabricated from sketch to finished piece.

Techniques include bezel and prong setting, mixed-metal construction, and sculptural casting. Recurring stones include labradorite, dendritic opal, amethyst, mystic topaz, ruby, and emerald.

Teardrop Kitten Series

One silhouette, many stones.

A signature seated-cat pendant, in which an oval head frames a single teardrop cabochon. Each is cast in a different stone and carries its own character.

ii. Sumi-e Ink Painting

Original narrative compositions in the East Asian sumi-e tradition.

Brush, ink, and selective watercolor on paper and fabric, employing wet and dry brushwork, wash gradation, and gestural line. All subject matter is original.

iii. Shodo Calligraphy

Japanese brush calligraphy, completed in a single take as both performance and object.

Kanji and Chinese characters rendered in gyosho and related scripts at gallery scale.

Exhibited Heike Monogatari opening verse, Birmingham Bloomfield Art Center Student Showcase.

iv. Origami

Folded by hand.

A lighter practice of animals, birds, and creatures, each folded from a single sheet. These are studies in patience, undertaken between larger works.

02 · Commissions

Made to order.

Bespoke pieces are created in close consultation with the person who will carry them, beginning with a single stone or name and ending in a finished object. A selection of past commissions is shown below.

JewelrySun & Moon, a pair made for a couple

HankoFour name seals, carved by hand

A hanko 判子 is a personal name seal carved into a small block of stone. Inked in vermilion and pressed to paper, it stands in for a handwritten signature across much of East Asia. Each one here is cut by hand, rendering a name in characters chosen for both their sound and their meaning.

Shodo CalligraphyCustom brushwork, commissioned to order

Commissioned shodō 書道: a name, a word, or a line of verse, brushed by hand in a chosen script and then sealed. New commissions are welcome.

Design for Six Sigma · Black Belt · General Motors

Improving SDV2 Power Management Specifications

The GB-5280 Partial Network specification was difficult to read, test, and trace. This caused friction between architects and test engineers, and added cost through repeated clarification cycles. The project measured the problem, redesigned the specification format, and verified the improvement with the people who use it.

01 · Define & Measure

  • Conducted a Voice of Customer survey across the specification consumer community.
  • Baseline scores for GB-5280 established quantified targets: testability 5.82 / 10 and traceability 5.64 / 10.

02 · Analyze

Four specification format concepts were evaluated against the survey findings:

  • Pseudocode
  • Graphics
  • Excel
  • Gherkin / BDD feature files

03 · Improve

  • Gherkin syntax for behavioral requirement language, readable by architects and directly testable.
  • Excel for the activation criteria sheet (SDV-8280A).
  • Word with supporting diagrams for foundational Partial Network content (SDV-8280).

Case Study · Systems Engineering Take-Home Exercise · 45 Minutes

Requirements & Verification for an Autonomous Tracking System

The scenario was a startup building a safety-critical autonomous outdoor system, with a customer demo in six weeks. The team was strong, but system-level requirements and verification were loose. Tracking was lost after vibration or occlusion, calibration took about 25 minutes with a senior engineer, logs were inconsistent, and ownership between teams was unclear. The brief asked for judgment and prioritization over completeness.

Priority requirements

AreaRequirementOwnerVerification
TrackingIdentify objects of at least 21 × 10 × 9 cm at up to 1 mileControlsDemonstration
TrackingTrack with ±1° positional error on both axes, against targets accelerating at 10 m/s²ControlsDemonstration
TrackingReacquire a lost target within 500 msControlsDemonstration
ReliabilityWithstand vibration up to 30 Hz and impulse up to 50 N·s for 5 hoursMechanicalMeasurement
OperationalFull functionality for 5 hours on one charge; total weight under 400 lb for a two-person carryElectrical · MechanicalDemonstration
OperationalAutonomous calibration routine, runnable by operatorsSoftware · ControlsDemonstration
DiagnosticLogs include timestamps, metadata, system state, and sensor dataSoftwareObservation

Verification plan

  • Scenario 1: Tracking. A Gazebo simulation with targets of varied size, distance, and acceleration, plus cover and debris to force reacquisition. This is followed by a physical test bench to capture friction, heat, and degradation that simulation misses.
  • Scenario 2: Reliability. A design review of structural natural frequencies, then a vibration test harness run to failure to begin tracking MTBF.
  • One extended tracking run doubles as the 5-hour endurance test.

Gaps, risks & assumptions

  • Gaps: interface control documents for shared requirements, subsystem decomposition, and safety requirements.
  • Risks: performance targets may be too strict, real-world use cases may not be covered, and failure modes are not yet addressed.
  • Assumptions: values are estimates to be refined with stakeholders; operator skill and training are still unknown.

First week

  • Centralize systems engineering documentation and link all tracked engineering work to a system-level requirement.
  • Hold an alignment session with the mechanical, electrical, controls, software, and test leads. The session would confirm current compliance, negotiate requirements, and pressure-test the six-week timeline.

Deliberately not formalized yet

  • The full calibration sequence depends on operator interfaces and final use cases.
  • Heavy process documentation is deferred: systems engineering should bring clarity, not paperwork.
  • Tracking decomposition and failure modes depend on solution choices, such as the sensor selection.
